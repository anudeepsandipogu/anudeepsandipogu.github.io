Principles

Hypotheses, not commandments. Last revised October 2026.

These are things I've noticed that keep being true. Several contradict each other. I've stopped trying to fix that.

Identity and values

  • Don't confuse optionality with indecision. Keeping doors open is a strategy. Refusing to walk through any of them is something else. I mix these up often enough that the principle exists.
  • A career is part of a life, not the other way around. I don't want one that requires postponing having a life until some future version of myself has earned it.
  • Notice what you want when nobody is watching. Most of my ambitions change shape under an audience. The ones that don't are probably mine.
  • You don't need a five-year plan. You need a direction and enough honesty to change it. Plans mostly serve to make uncertainty feel rude.

Thinking

  • If something looks stupid, assume a hidden constraint. People are rarely irrational; they're usually optimizing for something I can't see. Finding that thing is the whole game.
  • Ask what happens when it gets cheap. Most technological change is described in terms of what the technology can do. The interesting question is what people stop doing once it costs nothing.
  • Be suspicious of things that are very easy to measure. They attract all the attention, whether or not they matter. The important things — taste, trust, belonging — keep refusing to be metrics.
  • Write it down before deciding you understand it. Understanding is quiet about its own absence. Writing is not.

Learning

  • Learn enough fundamentals that tools stop feeling magical. Magical tools make you a passenger. Boring fundamentals make you dangerous, slowly.
  • If you cannot explain what you built, you don't understand it yet. The demo went well is not the same sentence as I know how this works.
  • Read outside your field before your field becomes your worldview. Engineers see incentives everywhere and call it insight. Every field does this. The cure is other fields.
  • Finish small things. Unfinished large things teach you about ambition. Finished small things teach you about the work. I have too many of the former.

Work

  • Why do smart people optimise for careers they don't particularly want? Not a principle, exactly. But I ask it whenever a prestigious path looks appealing, and the appeal usually decreases.
  • Build systems that make the desired behaviour ordinary. Discipline is a rumour about other people. Arrangement beats willpower: what you see first, what's already open, who's around.
  • Enough money to be free is a number. Rich is a treadmill. I want the number. The treadmill looks exhausting and, worse, boring.

Technology

  • Capability gets the demo; everything else decides the outcome. Workflows, trust, regulation, who benefits. The model is the easy part often enough that I now assume it.
  • Follow the data center, not the press release. Where the concrete is being poured tells you what people actually believe.

Life

  • Keep some parts of life useless. Football, long meals, wandering cities. The moment everything serves a goal, the goals get worse. I don't fully believe the mechanism, but I believe the observation.
  • Travel slowly enough to become slightly inconvenient. Convenience preserves the tourist. Inconvenience introduces you to residents, landlords, queues, ordinary Tuesdays.
  • Serendipity needs unscheduled time and repeated places. It doesn't visit calendars. It visits cafes you go to twice a week.
