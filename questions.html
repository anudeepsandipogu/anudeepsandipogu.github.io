Questions

The more honest page. Unresolved on purpose.

I keep this list because confusion evaporates if unwritten. None of these have answers I'm willing to defend.

Values and the good life

  • Am I discovering my values or constructing them? The difference matters, and I can't tell which one I'm doing.
  • Can an absurdist view of life coexist with taking values seriously? Camus says yes, in effect. I'm still checking whether I actually live that way.
  • How much of what I want is mine? Family, IIT, prestige hierarchies — all of them installed preferences before I was old enough to object.
  • What does freedom require — fewer obligations, or obligations I chose? These feel very different at 2am than at noon.
  • Ambition and a quiet life both appeal to me. Do I want both, or do I want to be seen wanting both?
  • What does a good week look like, concretely? Hours for work, people, health, reading, doing nothing. The abstract version of this question is useless; the weekly version is oddly hard.

Thinking and learning

  • When does connecting ideas across fields produce insight, and when is it just an analogy that flatters me?
  • Does writing in public clarify thinking, or does it freeze half-formed thoughts into positions I then defend?
  • How do you get depth in anything while staying interested in everything? My reading list suggests I haven't solved this.
  • Is philosophy something I actually practice, or something I perform? I worry about this more than I'd like.

Work

  • Why do smart people optimise for careers they don't particularly want? I watch it happen constantly, including, at times, to me.
  • Do I want a career in strategy, or do I simply like the idea of being the person who thinks about the big picture? Uncomfortably open question.
  • Is business school a way to buy perspective, or a way to postpone choosing? Possibly both. Possibly the postponement is the point and I'm dressing it up.
  • What number is "enough money to be free," actually? Nobody answers this numerically, which makes me suspect the question is doing emotional work the number can't.
  • Maybe the opposite of burnout isn't working less. Maybe it's caring about something else. Still testing this one.

Technology and companies

  • AI may make knowing how to use software less valuable and knowing what is worth asking more valuable. If so, what should anyone study? I study engineering, so this isn't abstract for me.
  • Why do good models die in hospitals while mediocre software spreads everywhere? The answer is probably incentives and workflow, but "probably" is doing a lot of work there.
  • Who captures the value when intelligence gets cheap — the model builders, the infrastructure owners, or whoever owns distribution? The concrete being poured suggests an answer I don't fully trust.
  • How much of corporate strategy is reasoning versus post-hoc storytelling? Having watched both, the ratio is worse than the literature admits.

France, culture, place

  • France has an unusually interesting answer to how much of life should belong to work. Is it a better answer, or just a different one that appeals to my current fatigue?
  • Am I romanticizing Europe from a distance? Winter, paperwork, and ordinary Tuesdays are the test. I haven't taken it yet.
  • How much of a person is their place? Move me to Lyon and something changes — taste, pace, what counts as serious. How much of "me" survives the move is an empirical question.
  • What does it mean to belong somewhere without becoming parochial? I want roots and movement. These negotiate poorly.
