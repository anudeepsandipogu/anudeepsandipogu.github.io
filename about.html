About

I'm an Engineering Design / Biomedical Design student at IIT Madras, in my early 20s, transitioning from university into professional life.

So far I've worked across engineering, biomedical AI, product design, research, and strategy/operations-type projects. That range was deliberate. I'm trying to understand the shape of work that fits me, rather than picking a label early and optimizing inside it.

I'm increasingly interested in the intersection of technology × business × strategy × economics × culture × society — specifically, how technological and economic changes reshape companies, careers, cities, and how people live.

In practice that means: technology strategy, corporate strategy, emerging industries, AI and its infrastructure, product and systems thinking, and how technology changes organizations. Eventually I want to operate at the intersection of technology, business, and societal problems — moving between engineering, technology, strategy, business, and entrepreneurship rather than locking into one identity.

I don't mean this as indecision to be fixed. I mean it as exploration. I care about financial security, but also about autonomy, time, intellectual stimulation, travel, relationships, family, physical activity, and having enough space to think. I want a career that preserves optionality.

I'm considering business school in Europe, particularly France. I'm not simply interested in the credential. I'm interested in what kind of environment and culture I want to build my adult life around — how different societies define a good life, and what that implies for ambition, work, and status.

How I think

I naturally think in systems. The questions I return to:

  • What is the underlying system here?
  • What assumptions are hidden?
  • What values produced this behaviour?
  • What incentives are shaping this?
  • What happens if this trend continues?
  • What does this look like at a larger historical or societal scale?
  • Am I solving the right problem?
  • Is this what I value, or someone else's definition of success?

A personal notebook I'm keeping in public. Principles I'm testing, questions I haven't answered, notes on what I'm reading and building. It will be wrong in places. That's intentional — I'm trying to preserve uncertainty rather than manufacture certainty to sound authoritative.
