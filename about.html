About

I'm 21, studying Engineering Design at IIT Madras. So far I've worked on biomedical AI, product design, and a few strategy and operations projects. The range wasn't a plan so much as curiosity with a calendar.

Engineering taught me that things have underlying mechanisms. I've spent the last couple of years applying that instinct to everything else: why a company behaves this way, why a city looks this way, why a society values this, why a person wants this.

One day I think about AI infrastructure. The next I wonder why French people think about work so differently. I don't see these as separate interests. They're the same question at different scales: an individual, an organization, an industry, a country. AI → jobs → companies → labour markets → what a society considers a good life. France → work culture → institutions → history → ideas about adulthood.

I'm interested in strategy, institutions, and culture, and I may apply to business school in Europe. Not for the credential, particularly. France interests me because there seems to be something there about life, aesthetics, and intellectual seriousness that is worth understanding on its own terms — a different conception of what adulthood is for.

Incidentally: I read Kafka and Tolstoy, follow Liverpool and Formula 1, like designing things, and care about having enough money to be free rather than becoming rich as an end in itself. I want time for travel, sport, reading, friends, and family. I am interested in philosophy but wary of turning it into an identity.

How I think

I look for the system first, then try to notice the people living inside it. The system explains the behaviour; the people explain why the system isn't the whole story. I forget the second half regularly.

Questions I return to: what is everyone assuming here? What would this look like ten times larger, or twenty years later? Is this what I want, or what I'm supposed to want?

What this site is

An unfinished map. Some of what's here I believed strongly a year ago and now find slightly embarrassing. I've left the contradictions in. The point is to watch a worldview being built, not to present one.
