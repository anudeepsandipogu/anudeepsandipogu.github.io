How I travel, and places that pull at me. I live in Chennai.

How I try to travel

  • Stay long enough for the novelty to wear off. The first week teaches you about yourself. The second teaches you about the place.
  • Watch the ordinary: grocery shops, lunch hours, how evenings work. Sights show what a place achieved. Habits show what it values.
  • Learn enough of the language to listen. Even bad French changes what people tell you.
  • Write one page per place, late in the stay. What did this place make easy, and what did it make hard?
  • Be a guest, not a tourist.

The plan, once I'm working: two or three months abroad every year.

Money across borders

Purchasing power parity tells you how far an income goes locally. It doesn't tell you how far it goes globally. Cars, electronics, flights, education and global assets are priced in world markets. So I compare four things: local purchasing power, savings, exchange rates, and public services.

From home

Chennai runs on improvisation. Where institutions are thin, people fill the gaps. That brings warmth, and it's tiring. Much of Europe makes the opposite bet: strong institutions, little improvisation. Which one I'd rather live inside is the question behind much of this site.
