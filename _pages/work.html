Turning a solar forecast into money

Strategy analyst intern · Honeywell, Bengaluru · Jan – Jun 2026

Six months on strategy for India and Asia-Pacific. The work I learned most from started with a model that predicts global horizontal irradiance: how much sunlight reaches a solar plant. I built its dashboard. Then I built a financial model that carried the prediction through. Sunlight becomes generation. A shortfall in generation becomes a contractual penalty for Honeywell and the plant's owner. I ran four scenarios, from baseline to aggressive, over seven financial years.

A forecast has no value by itself. Its value is the decision it changes, in rupees. I also worked on the early stages of what became Honeywell's Renewable Energy Stack. That was a team's work; I was one of the people involved early.

A Hyperloop for freight

Junior engineer · Avishkar Hyperloop, Centre for Innovation, IIT Madras · Sep 2024 – Jul 2025

Most Hyperloop teams work on the pod. Our socio-economic team asked whether a freight-only Hyperloop along India's Golden Quadrilateral would improve how the country moves goods. We compared it with road, rail and air on cost, time and CO₂. A machine-learning model picked the best mode for each shipment in the dataset.

ModePredicted best, share of cases
Hyperloop74.90%
Rail24.29%
Road0.66%
Air0.40%

Model output under the study's assumptions, not a forecast of real traffic.

It won first prize in the socio-economic research category at European Hyperloop Week 2025 in Groningen. What I took from it: whether infrastructure gets built depends mostly on economics and institutions.
