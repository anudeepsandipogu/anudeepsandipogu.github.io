Travel

Notes on places. Travel as inhabiting systems, not collecting destinations.

For me travel is less about checking places off and more about temporarily living inside a different system — noticing how people work, eat, gather, argue, rest — and what that reveals about my own assumptions.

I'm based in India (Chennai / IIT Madras). My strongest interest right now is France and Europe more broadly: how different societies define a good life, and what that implies for ambition, work-life expectations, status, aesthetics, food, cities, and intellectual life.

How I try to travel

  • Stay long enough for novelty to wear off. The first week tells you about yourself; the second tells you about the place.
  • Observe the ordinary: grocery stores, commutes, lunch hours, how evenings work. Tourist sites tell you little about a system.
  • Ask what is considered normal here that would be strange at home — around work, ambition, food, relationships, time.
  • Learn enough of the language to be polite and to listen. Even basic French changes what people tell you.
  • Write one page per place: what did this system optimize for, and what did it trade off?

Questions I'm carrying about France / Europe

  • What does France optimize for that India or the US doesn't — and what does it sacrifice for that?
  • How do attitudes toward ambition, status, and work differ, concretely — in hours, in conversation, in what a "serious person" is expected to do?
  • What role do cities, food, aesthetics, and intellectual traditions play in everyday life versus special occasions?
  • Am I romanticizing Europe from a distance? What would living there — paperwork, winter, ordinary Tuesdays — actually feel like?
  • What kind of environment do I want to build my adult life around, beyond any single school or job?
