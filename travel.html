Travel

Notes on places. Kept slowly.

I prefer slow travel to collecting destinations. Living somewhere long enough is different from visiting it. Eventually you stop seeing the country and start noticing your own assumptions.

I'm based in Chennai. My strongest pull right now is France — not as a career move primarily, but because there seems to be something there about life, culture, and adulthood worth understanding firsthand.

How I try to travel

  • Stay long enough for novelty to wear off. The first week tells you about yourself; the second tells you about the place.
  • Watch the ordinary: grocery stores, lunch hours, how evenings work. Sights tell you what a place achieved. Habits tell you what it values.
  • Learn enough of the language to listen. Even bad French changes what people tell you — effort registers.
  • One page per place, written late in the stay. What did this system make easy, and what did it make hard?

Questions I'm carrying about France

  • How much of life should belong to work? France has an unusually interesting answer. Is it better, or does it just appeal to my current fatigue? Unresolved, obviously.
  • What do aesthetics do in French daily life — food, streets, argument — that elsewhere gets filed under luxury? I suspect something load-bearing is hiding there.
  • Am I romanticizing from a distance? Winter, paperwork, and ordinary Tuesdays are the test. Not taken yet.
  • If I lived in Lyon for a year, what would change about me — pace, taste, what counts as serious? How much of "me" is actually just Chennai?

I think of European order as the opposite bet: institutions thick, improvisation thin. Which constraints I'd choose to live inside is the open question behind most of this site.
